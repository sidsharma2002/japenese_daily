Read as a warm, clear Japanese teacher talking to one learner: relaxed, friendly, steady pace, slow and clear on the Japanese words.

<excited> A friend says they cannot come to your party. What do you say back? Here is the word, and why it works.

<confident> First, say ざんねんです. It means, that is too bad. Now break it open. The first kanji means remain. The second kanji means thought. So it is a thought that stays with you.

<confident> Second, look at the first kanji alone. It reads, のこる. It means, to remain. Like, one cake remains on the table. One piece is left behind, just like that thought.

<confident> Third, the second kanji shows up again. Say, ねんのため. It means, just in case. Like, just in case, I will write it down. A small thought, to be safe.

<excited> So remember this. Remain plus thought equals regret. One kanji pair, three useful phrases.
