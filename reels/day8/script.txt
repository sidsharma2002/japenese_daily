<excited> This English-looking word does not mean what you think. In Japan, it means complaint.

<confident> Picture a shop counter. A staff member tells the manager, おきゃくさまから、くれーむがきました. That is, we got a complaint from a customer.

<confident> The word comes from English claim. But in English, a claim is a demand, or a statement that something is true, like an insurance claim. Japanese kept the sound, and the meaning moved.

<confident> Here is the trap. Say it to an English speaker, and they will not hear complaint. To complain in Japanese, say くれーむをつけます. That is, to lodge a complaint. There is also くじょう, a more formal word for complaint.

<excited> Say it out loud, くれーむ. Complaint, never claim.
