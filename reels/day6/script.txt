Calm and direct, like a friend correcting you kindly. Slow on the Japanese.

<excited> Someone offers you tea, and you say, いいです. They may hear, no thank you.

<confident> Here is the slip. A host holds out a cup, and you say, いいです, thinking, that sounds good. You meant yes.

<confident> But after an offer, いいです can also read as, I am fine, no need. The host smiles and puts the cup down.

<confident> The fix is simple. Say, はい、おねがいします, which is, yes please. Or say, いただきます, I will gladly have it. One exception. If someone asks, いいですか, is this okay, then いいです really is yes.

<excited> So when it is an offer, say yes with real words, not with いいです.

<upbeat> Save this, you will want it later.
